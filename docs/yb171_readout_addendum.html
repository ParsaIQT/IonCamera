Yb171 EMCCD Readout Addendum

On 17 Oct the camera half of the readout model was measured directly, from dark frames. It confirms the camera parameters fitted to run 106415, traces that run's 0.33 e⁻ background to clock-induced charge at a 3.3 µs vertical shift, and supplies fixed hyper-parameters so the SPAM pmfs only have to fit the ion.

Data: ioncam characterization 20261017-113225_DU897-BB (iXon Ultra 897, s/n 12729; 677 settings, 3 million samples each, shutter closed, −70 °C), readout plan 20261013-141940, ioncam event log 13–17 Oct. Audience: the lab and the analysis code in RandomIQTApplets/yb171_emccd_readout (ybreadout). Prepared 17 Oct 2026 with Claude Code.

1In short

  • The camera parameters are now measured, not only fitted. Dark frames reproduce the camera fitted to run 106415: read noise σ 8.665 against 8.653 ADU, g 38.6 against 38.3 ADU/e⁻, real EM gain 164.5 against ≈ 164 at a set 200, zero level 499.7 against 498.7–499.2 ADU.
  • Run 106415's background is clock-induced charge (CIC). Its λbg = 0.3311 ± 0.007 e⁻/shot (0.338 in the empty ROI) equals the full-gain CIC of one 4×4 super-pixel at a 3.3 µs vertical shift, 0.331 ± 0.003. At 0.9 µs, the speed the report names, it would be 0.044. The fitted serial CIC, 0.357 ± 0.032, also matches 3.3 µs (0.43) and not 0.9 µs (0.18).
  • The "0.9 µs" most likely comes from beta5.py. It calls SetFKVShiftSpeed(2), and index 2 is 0.9 µs on this camera, but that call sets the fast-kinetics speed and has no effect in image mode. The speed this camera recommends at normal clock voltage is 3.3 µs, its slowest.
  • CIC costs about two points of SPAM error. At the report's threshold (117 ADU above B) the camera alone reads an empty ROI as bright 2.4% of the time, most of P(B|D) = 3.28%. At 0.5 µs that falls to 0.08–0.16%, and the predicted mean error at the same Λ = 55 falls from 5.1% to 2.8–3.0%. The report's own "no background" row gives 3.03%.
  • ioncam has armed the readout at 0.5 µs since 13 Oct. The r106415 calibration (serial CIC 0.357, background prior 0.33) does not describe data taken that way. Recalibrate before analysing them.
  • For the SPAM pmfs: fix σ, the serial CIC and the CIC per vertical-shift speed; fit B per ROI; give g a prior from the same session; use the real gain (≈ 168) in the cascade. The calibration fit drops from 13 to 8 free parameters, its worst-determined combination goes from ×8.5 to ×2.6 error inflation, and a pmf costs 0.33 ms instead of 6.6 ms with the camera factor cached.

3The background is clock-induced charge

Clock-induced charge is made when charge is clocked. Charge made while rows shift through the image and storage areas passes the whole EM register and gets full gain: that is the report's background term. Charge made inside the EM register gets only part of the gain: that is the report's serial CIC. On this camera both grow with the time each row shift takes, and the speed the camera recommends at normal clock voltage, 3.3 µs, is its slowest.

Why 3.3 µs

  • The amount. λbg = 0.3311 ± 0.007 (ROI 2: 0.3381 ± 0.007) against λCIC(3.3 µs) = 0.331 ± 0.003. The AQROI's columns (x 347–355) show 8–12% more dark events than the measured region (§9.8), so 0.36 ± 0.02 there: still within 10%. No other speed comes within a factor of 2.8.
  • The serial CIC. It also rises with the shift time. The report's 0.357 ± 0.032 is 2σ from the 3.3 µs value (0.43 ± 0.014), 3.4σ from 1.7 µs (0.25) and 5.5σ from 0.9 µs (0.18). Full-gain CIC and light are indistinguishable in SPAM data (both are Poisson electrons at full gain), but the serial CIC is a fingerprint of the shift speed.
  • The source of "0.9 µs". beta5.py line 80: self.sdk.SetFKVShiftSpeed(2). On this camera the speeds are 0.3, 0.5, 0.9, 1.7, 3.3 µs, so index 2 reads as 0.9 µs; the call applies only to fast kinetics. beta5 runs live view on an internal trigger, so it may not have configured the SPAM runs at all.
  • The case against. ioncam's README lists 0.5 µs among the lab's Solis readout settings. If run 106415 was configured in Solis at 0.5 µs, CIC was 0.011–0.030 and the background was mostly light. The equal background in both ROIs does not decide it: CIC is uniform, and so could be scatter over two neighbouring super-pixels.
Settle it

Fit one SPAM run taken through ioncam since 13 Oct (armed at 0.5 µs; §8 step 4) with analyze_artiq_spam.py. λbg ≈ 0.01–0.05 with cs ≈ 0.04–0.11 confirms CIC. λbg still near 0.33 means light, and then Rec 2 stands. The run's own camera settings, if recorded, decide it directly.

  • Conditioning is the main gain. With everything free, λB, g, the serial CIC and the background share one poorly determined direction (variance inflation 20–23 for each). Fixing the camera leaves only the physical pair αdb/εd, which both put light into dark-prepared shots. Fits converge faster and small data sets stay stable.
  • λB is pinned only when g is. The data fix the product g·λB. Populations depend on the count distribution, which the product fixes, so a free g costs them nothing. λB in photo-electrons and Λ = λB/αbd need g; use a same-session prior.
  • The leak rates gain 8–17% at fixed shot count. The preparation errors barely move. Moving to 0.5 µs helps αdb more (×1.4 at the same shot count), because the dark shots are cleaner.

Speed

Benchmark on one core (numpy): an emulation of the report's roi_pmf (M = 4096 points, 536-stage cascade) takes 6.6 ms, of which 5.6 ms is the cascade and camera factor. With the camera factor cached, the ion part and the inverse FFT take 0.33 ms (20× faster), with identical output. A kernel matrix K[x, n] = P(x | n full-gain electrons) of 4096 × 80 turns a pmf into one matrix-vector product, 0.09 ms, given the ion's photon-number distribution. Fewer free parameters also mean fewer evaluations per fit.

7Changes for ybreadout

  1. Split the background. λbg = λCIC(shift speed, binning) + λlight. λCIC comes from the camera; λlight ≥ 0 is fitted, or set to 0 after laser-on frames without an ion.
  2. Make the camera a per-ROI object (B, σ, g, cs, λCIC, G, N, npix), loaded from a dark calibration at the exact readout (§8 step 2), with §5 as the fallback and the priors.
  3. Cache ψ and ψs per (g, G). With g free, tabulate the cascade once over θ = ω·g/G and interpolate: ψ depends on ω and g only through θ.
  4. Cache the camera factor per ROI. B can stay free at no cost: it is the phase factor exp(iω(B − x0)).
  5. Use the real gain in the cascade: G ≈ 168 at a set 200, p = G1/536 − 1 = 0.0096.
  6. Population inference: with λCIC fixed, refit only each ion's brightness per data set. Drop the 30% log-normal prior on λbg: it is centred on the 3.3 µs value (0.33) and would pull 0.5 µs data up thirtyfold.
  7. Thresholds: anything that still thresholds needs a new one. The optimal threshold is about 33 ADU above B at 0.5 µs, against 117–119 at 3.3 µs.
  8. Crosstalk: the 1.03% belongs to one ion beside an empty ROI. ioncam's readout plan reports crosstalk_matrix, containment and background_contamination for the current geometry.
  9. Pixel-resolved readout (Rec 3): the frame-to-frame common mode is 0.8–1.0 ADU rms and shared by all pixels of a frame. In a 16-pixel sum it adds about 18% to the read-noise variance; model one offset per frame, or take it from dark pixels of the same frame.
# ybreadout sketch: the camera is fixed, only the ion changes between evaluations
import numpy as np
from functools import lru_cache
from ybreadout.ion import mmpp_pgf
from ybreadout.emccd import cascade_cf, quantisation_cf

M, N = 4096, 536
w = 2 * np.pi * np.arange(M // 2 + 1) / M

@lru_cache(maxsize=16)
def cascade(g, G=168.0):
    return cascade_cf(w * g / G, G, N, serial=True)          # psi, psi_s: 5.6 ms, once

@lru_cache(maxsize=64)
def camera(roi, sigma, g, c_s, lam_cic, G=168.0, n_pix=1):
    psi, psi_s = cascade(g, G)
    cam = np.exp(-0.5 * (sigma * w) ** 2 + c_s * (psi_s - 1) + lam_cic * (psi - 1))
    return psi, cam * quantisation_cf(w, n_pix)               # per ROI, once

def roi_pmf(cam_key, B, x0, state, lam, a_bd, a_db, lam_light=0.0):
    psi, cam = camera(*cam_key)
    cf = cam * np.exp(1j * w * (B - x0)) * mmpp_pgf(psi, lam, a_bd, a_db, state)
    if lam_light:
        cf = cf * np.exp(lam_light * (psi - 1))
    return np.fft.irfft(np.conj(cf), n=M)                     # 0.33 ms

10Open questions and caveats

  • The shift speed of runs 106415 and 95989. Run 95989's 2.0 e⁻ background at 3.3 µs would be 0.33 e⁻ of CIC and 1.7 e⁻ of light, consistent with the room light the report saw.
  • Serial CIC at the real readout. It was measured on a 144 px wide region on an internal trigger; the experiment reads a 2 × 2 super-pixel frame on an external trigger. Serial CIC may depend on how long the register clocks between reads, and the report's 0.357 against 0.429 here may be that. §8 step 2 measures it.
  • 4×4 values below 3.3 µs are scaled from 1×1. The two scalings differ by up to 2.6× for λCIC at 0.5 µs; predicted SPAM moves only 2.8 → 3.0%. One short run per speed removes the doubt (§11).
  • Column dependence. Dark events vary ±20% across the sensor (§9.8).
  • g. It follows the recent pixel rate (§9.1), and S is the factory value, so the real gain carries S's error.
  • B. It has a column pattern, moves about 1 ADU with the shift speed at 5 MHz and up to 30 ADU between readout rates: fit it per ROI and data set.
  • Temperature. Run 106415's sensor temperature is not known here; CIC is 14% higher at −60 °C.

11Settings and follow-up runs

  • Readout: 5 MHz, pre-amp 3, EM 200 (RealGain), 4×4, normal clock voltage, 0.5 µs vertical shift; 0.3 µs is marginally better. In ioncam, put vs_speed_us = 0.5 in the [readout] section of the configuration file. The value 0 means "the camera's recommended speed", which is 3.3 µs on this camera; the 17 Oct session ran with 0. A value set in the GUI or over RPC is not saved.
  • Live view: if a stable g matters right after arming, run live view at 5 MHz as well ([live] hs_speed_mhz = 5.0), or wait a few minutes after arming.
  • EM gain: 200 is fine. EM 300 lowers σ/g by a third (0.15 e⁻ at 5 MHz) at the cost of faster register ageing. Above 300 needs advanced EM gain.
